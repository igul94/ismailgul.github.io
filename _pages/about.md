I am a Ph.D. Candidate in the Department of Civil, Environmental and Ocean Engineering at Stevens Institute of Technology.

My research focuses on improving precipitation monitoring and forecasting by integrating ground-based observations, weather radar, and numerical weather prediction (NWP) models to support flood risk assessment. My work aims to address observational gaps and uncertainties in precipitation estimates.

As part of my doctoral research, I have developed and deployed low-cost IoT-based sensors for real-time rainfall and water level monitoring in New Jersey through the [NJFloodNet project](https://web.stevens.edu/ismart/land_products/NJFloodNet.html), with ongoing efforts to expand the network statewide. I have also developed a precipitation nowcasting framework that combines MRMS observations with HRRR model predictions to improve short-term rainfall forecasts.

I also contributed to the deployment and data system setup of a dual-polarization X-band weather radar at Stevens Institute of Technology as part of the [Stevens High-resolution Atmospheric Radar Project (SHARP)](https://web.stevens.edu/ismart/atmosphere_products/sharp.html) ([Stevens News](https://www.stevens.edu/news/stevens-institute-of-technology-installs-advanced-x-band-weather-radar-to)). My current research focuses on evaluating its performance against the NEXRAD network. Ultimately, I aim to integrate these complementary observing systems to provide more accurate precipitation estimates and improve flood forecasting across the New York–New Jersey metropolitan area.
